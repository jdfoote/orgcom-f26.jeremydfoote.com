What is an Organization? Activities

Activity 1: Gallery Walk

Setup

Post each scenario card around the room. Students move through them with a marker or sticky dots (green = yes, red = no, yellow = unsure). Give about 10 minutes to visit all cards, then regroup to discuss the contested ones.

Discussion prompt after the walk: What features seemed to matter most in deciding? What kept coming up?

Scenario Cards

Card A: Apple Inc. 160,000 employees worldwide. Formal hierarchy, explicit goals, legal incorporation, products you can buy. Is it an organization?

Card B: Your Family Five people who live together, share finances, have meals together, and have unspoken roles (who does dishes, who picks up kids). Is it an organization?

Card C: The Purdue Student Body 50,000 students enrolled at Purdue. They all pay tuition, share a campus, and are governed by the same university rules — but most have never met each other. Is it an organization?

Card D: A Discord Fan Server 3,400 members who joined a server dedicated to a TV show. They share memes, debate plot points, and have volunteer moderators who enforce rules. There are no dues, no formal leadership, no stated goals. Is it an organization?

Card E: A Sunday Pickup Basketball Game 10 people who show up to the campus gym every Sunday morning to play basketball. No signup, no dues, no formal membership — just a recurring pattern. Is it an organization?

Card F: A Flash Mob 200 strangers who found each other online, coordinated to perform a choreographed dance in Union Station for 5 minutes, then dispersed and never interacted again. Is it an organization?

Card G: A Dormant Neighborhood Association Formally incorporated, has elected officers and bylaws, collects annual dues — but hasn’t held a meeting in 3 years and has no active members. Is it an organization?

Card H: The U.S. Federal Government 4 million employees, constitutional authority, explicit hierarchy, legal powers, and stated goals. Is it an organization?

Debrief Questions

  • Which ones were easiest to decide? Why?
  • Which were hardest? What made them hard?
  • What features seemed necessary for something to be an organization?
  • Did any of you change your mind after seeing what others marked?

Activity 2: Org Mapping

Instructions (individual, ~8 minutes)

On a blank piece of paper, map all the organizations you are currently part of — or have been part of recently. Include formal ones (jobs, clubs, sports teams, schools) and informal ones (family, friend groups, regular gatherings) if they feel organization-like to you.

For each one, note:

  • Its name / what it is
  • Roughly how many people are involved
  • Whether your membership is voluntary or involuntary
  • Whether the structure is formal (explicit rules, roles, hierarchy) or informal

Try to get at least 6–8 on your map.

Pair Share (5 minutes)

Compare maps with a partner:

  • What surprised you about your own list?
  • Are any of the same organizations on both of your maps?
  • Which one on your map do you know the most about from the inside?

Whole-Class Debrief

  • What types of organizations showed up most?
  • Did any organizations feel hard to place (voluntary vs. involuntary? formal vs. informal?)?
  • Look at your map: which organization would you most want to dig into for a 4-page analysis? Circle it.
Tip

The organization you circle today is a strong candidate for your final project.
