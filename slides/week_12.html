Emotion and rationality

  • Classic models treat organizations as rational: logic in, decisions out

  • Even bounded rationality treats rationality as the goal

  • Mumby & Putnam: bounded emotionality. Feelings, relationships, and intuition are part of how organizations work

  • Think about a recent decision you made. What role did emotion play in that decision?

Emotion as part of the job

  • Emotional labor: managing your feelings in the ways an organization requires, for the organization’s gain
    • Flight attendants, servers, retail, call centers
  • Emotional work: emotional situations as part of the job
    • Nurses, teachers, social workers

Surface acting vs. deep acting

  • Surface acting - displaying an emotion you don’t feel as requred

  • Deep acting - trying to actually feel the emotion you’re supposed to show

  • Where have you done each kind of acting? Which was more tiring?

Emotions at work

  • Our relationships with coworkers is hugely important

  • Emotional buzzing - emotions spread across workplaces

  • Workplace bullying - real trauma and stigma for targets

  • Compassion - a culture of compassion can help people cope with stress and burnout

  • Have you seen one person’s mood change a whole team? What happened?

Emotional intelligence

  • The idea: some people are better at understanding and managing emotions, their own and others’

  • Very popular with managers and corporate trainers

  • Criticized for weak research evidence, and for being hard to distinguish from other traits

  • Why do you think it’s so popular anyway?

Stress and burnout

  • Stressors - aspects of the environment that create strain: workload, role conflict, role ambiguity
  • Burnout has three dimensions:
    • Emotional exhaustion (the core)
    • Reduced personal accomplishment
    • Depersonalization - treating people as cases or objects
  • Feeling for people (empathic concern) is healthier than catching their feelings (emotional contagion)

Coping with burnout

  • Problem-centered - change the stressor (ask for a clear job description)

  • Appraisal-centered - change how you see the situation

  • Emotion-centered - deal with the feelings (exercise, venting, time off)

  • Participation in decision making reduces role conflict and role ambiguity

  • Pick a stressor in your life as a student. What would each kind of coping look like?

Social support

  • Emotional - empathy, caring, listening
  • Informational - facts and advice
  • Instrumental - practical help (covering a shift, cooking dinner)
  • Different people give different kinds:
    • Supervisors: informational and instrumental
    • Coworkers: informational and emotional
    • Friends and family: emotional and instrumental
  • Think of a time when you needed support. Who do you go to for each kind of support? Why?

  • How can managers encourage employees to express their emotions honestly while still maintaining professionalism in the workplace?
  • As a student, how do you cope with stress and burnout, and how does it compare to the chapter?

Reading Review Questions

  • What is the difference between emotional labor and emotional work?
  • What is the distinction between “emotional buzzing,” “co-rumination,” and healthy communication between coworkers
  • How would making more room for emotional expression in the workplace affect the organization? What are some potential benefits and drawbacks?
