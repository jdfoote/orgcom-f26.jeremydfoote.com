Basic Concepts

  • What is ideology in the organizational communication sense? Give an example of an ideological belief that operates inside a workplace.
  • What is hegemony?
  • How do organizations reproduce power and what role does communication play in that?

Basic Concepts (cont.)

  • What is the difference between overt and covert worker resistance? Give an example of each.
  • What are the types of control that organizations use to maintain power over workers?
  • What is “concertive control” and how does it differ from other forms of control?

Basic Concepts (cont.)

  • What is the argument that organizations are typically patriarchal?
  • Why do feminist scholars argue that it is useful to look at organizations through a gendered lens? In what ways do women experience organizations differently than men?

Basic Concepts (cont.)

  • What’s the difference between describing an organization and critiquing one? What does a critical approach let you see that a neutral description wouldn’t?
  • Can critical and feminist approaches coexist with the frameworks from earlier chapters (Classical, HR, Systems)? Or are they fundamentally incompatible?

Reading Review Questions

  • How can employees recognize and challenge unfair power dynamics in the workplace, especially when certain rules or expectations have become so normalized that people no longer question them?
  • How do organizations create or reinforce gender expectations?
  • Aren’t mascuine and feminine norms in a workplace complementary? Can’t an organization be logical and nurturing at the same time? Why or why not?

Reading Review Questions (cont.)

  • The textbook discusses challenging hegemony within organizations, but those in power likely won’t give it up easily. Is there a way to peacefully change an organization’s power structure without creating a lot of conflict?
  • Can resistance in an organization actually improve an organization or can it create more problems between employees and management?
  • How can organizations make employees accept or participate in systems of control without employees realizing they are being controlled?

Reading Review Questions (cont.)

  • Can resistance be achieved with just one person retaliating against the dominant class within the organization (e.g., whistleblowers)?
  • How can you tell the difference of a supportive team/work environment and one that is more toxic peer pressure where coworkers police eachother?
  • How can a critical or feminist researcher actively push for organizational change while still conducting objective and credible research?

## Reading Review Questions (cont.)

  • What makes concertive control theory a critical theory when it does not seem explicitly focused on domination of one group by another nor on the existence of power imbalances but instead seems concerned with providing a framework for the maintenance of organizations in the absence of hierarchy and power imbalance?
  • Can an organization truly be gender neutral, or will workplace communication and expectations always reflect some form of gender values?
